# Sources and scope: catalogue-only Pokémon releases

**Cutoff: 2026-11-22 UTC**, checked against the project host clock. `catalog-unavailable.json` describes official releases that Retro Pocket does **not** import, download, or play. Each record has a platform-prefixed ID, an empty `variants` array, and an app-specific `unavailable` reason. No ROM files, hashes, or ROM URLs are included. “Unavailable” in the app does not mean a game was never released, has been delisted, or is unplayable on its original hardware.

**Year** means the first documented release year for the listed platform/version in any region, including public regional releases but not prerelease betas. For add-ons, it is the year the add-on first became available, not necessarily when all story content shipped. For titles with regional names, the English name is preferred where one exists; romanizations or translated names are used for selected Japan-only releases. Publisher and platform-holder pages take priority.[3][25] Bulbapedia supplies regional releases and older material that publisher indexes omit.[4][5]

The 3DS Virtual Console rows include the 2016 Red/Green/Blue/Yellow releases (including the distinct Japanese and international Blue versions), 2017 Gold/Silver releases and 2018 Crystal release.[5][26][62] The 2026 FireRed and LeafGreen rows are standalone Switch reissues of the 2004 Game Boy Advance games; Nintendo confirms local wireless features on Switch and Switch 2, not a separate Switch 2 edition or Nintendo Classics subscription.[7][59] Original GB/GBC/GBA and DS releases are not duplicated here as original-platform rows.

Scarlet and Violet's free Switch 2 performance update is not a new edition.[68] Pokémon Friends is a Switch game compatible with Switch 2.[71] Pokémon Champions is a battle spinoff, available on both Switch systems from April 8, 2026 (publisher announcement date), with its mobile launch on June 17, 2026.[10][11] Pokémon Pokopia is a separate Switch 2 spinoff, released March 5, 2026.[7][109]

## Editions, expansions and bundles

- **Sword and Shield:** The two paid passes each require the matching base version. Each contains *The Isle of Armor* (June 17, 2020) and *The Crown Tundra* (October 22, 2020), which are not sold as standalone games.[112] The November 6, 2020 base-game-plus-pass releases are bundles.[44]
- **Scarlet and Violet:** The version-specific *Hidden Treasure of Area Zero* DLC includes *The Teal Mask* (September 2023) and *The Indigo Disk* (December 2023).[53][54][111] The November 2023 base-game-plus-DLC packs are bundles; the January 2024 epilogue requires the base game and DLC, not a separate title.[92][110]
- **Legends: Z-A:** The separate Switch 2 Edition row covers the enhanced release, also obtainable by upgrading the Switch version.[17] *Mega Dimension* is one paid DLC for either version; apparel shipped October 16, 2025, and the story content December 10, 2025.[19] The 2026 digital base-game-plus-DLC bundles are recorded on their respective platforms; the October 29 physical Switch 2 bundle shares the Switch 2 bundle row.[77][83][82] An upgrade entitlement is not listed as a game.
- **Pokopia:** The three-part paid Expansion Pass requires the base game; a storefront bundle contains both. Part 1, *Bubbly Basin*, was released in August 2026. Part 2, *Dazzling Decorations*, was dated December 9, 2026, and Part 3 was scheduled for 2027, so neither is recorded as a released installment.[29][78][80]

## Service status and omissions

- Wii Shop new purchases closed in 2019; Nintendo Wi-Fi Connection for Wii/DS ended in 2014.[39][75] The 3DS/Wii U eShops stopped new acquisitions in 2023 and most built-in online services ended in 2024.[38][40] These events do not disable offline play on original hardware.[38] Bank and Poké Transporter were exceptions to the general 2024 online shutdown; Bank (and Bank-to-HOME transfers) is now scheduled to end in February 2027.[38][95]
- Pokémon Duel, Pokémon Rumble Rush and Pokémon TCG Online have publisher-confirmed service closures. This is distinct from games that still run on their native platforms but have no Retro Pocket backend. Current store and server status was not individually audited for every older mobile/arcade game or in every region.[21][49][50]
- This is a broad spinoff selection, not an inventory of every licensed product. It omits most PC/browser ephemera, regional promotional and educational software (e.g. Sega Pico/Beena), event software, feature-phone/Japanese utility apps beyond the included examples, TV/news apps, demos, bonus discs, distribution cartridges, unofficial projects, and crossovers where Pokémon are guest characters (such as Super Smash Bros.).[4][5][104] Ordinary backwards compatibility, dual-version packs, language editions, patches, individual cosmetics and in-game events are not new rows. GB/GBC/GBA/DS originals are maintained separately; selected official reissues are named as such here.[62]
